What Happens When You Call the National Problem Gambling Helpline

CornerDay Team · 4 min read

The number gets mentioned everywhere — on betting slips, in apps, on this site — but almost nobody explains what actually happens when you dial it. That gap keeps a lot of people from ever calling. Here's what to expect.

National Problem Gambling Helpline 1-800-522-4700

Free, confidential, available 24 hours a day, every day.

Who answers

A trained person, not a bot and not law enforcement. Calls are typically answered by counselors or trained volunteers connected to your state's problem gambling network. Their job is to listen and point you toward help — not to judge, report, or pressure you into anything.

What they'll ask

Usually very little at first. Most calls start with something as simple as "what's going on?" You don't need a prepared summary, a diagnosis, or a specific amount you've lost. You can be vague, upset, or unsure why you're calling — that's a normal way to start.

What they won't do

  • They won't tell your family, employer, or anyone else you called
  • They won't require your real name
  • They won't lecture you or tell you how bad your situation is
  • They won't ask for money or try to sell you anything

What you can actually get out of the call

  • Someone to talk to in a moment that feels unmanageable alone
  • Referrals to local support groups or licensed counselors, if you want them
  • Information on self-exclusion programs for casinos and sportsbooks
  • Sometimes, pointers toward financial counseling for gambling-related debt

None of it is mandatory. You can call once, get information, and never call again. You can call at 3am just to say it out loud to someone. There's no version of the call that "commits" you to anything.

"I put off calling for two years because I thought I had to be sure it was 'bad enough' first. The person who answered didn't ask me to prove anything — they just asked how they could help."

If you're not ready to call yet

That's okay. Tracking your betting honestly, reading about the patterns that matter, or just sitting with the thought that something needs to change are all real steps too. The number will still be there when you're ready — save it somewhere you'll actually see it again.
